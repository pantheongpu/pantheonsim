# Matrix numerics per profile: what is measured, published, inferred, unknown

Issue #664, third item. For every profile that has matrix instructions: the sum its tensor core or matrix core makes, and what that rests on.
The classes are the profile loader's (`provenance:` in each profile, [nvidia/docs/profiles.md](../../nvidia/docs/profiles.md)) and `vgpu info --gpu <id>` prints them:

| Class | Means here |
|---|---|
| **measured** | a card of that model was run by us (card, driver, toolkit, date in the profile's `evidence:`) and the simulator's bits are the card's |
| **documented** / published | a paper or a vendor document gives the rule; no card of ours (and no recorded vectors) checked it. No profile claims this class for matrix numerics today |
| **inferred** | not known of this device itself: carried over from a measured device of the same compute capability, or reproduced against vectors that *others* recorded on cards of the family (Khattak and Mikaitis, arXiv 2512.07004), never run by us on that card |
| **unknown** | none of the above: the value is the simulator's placeholder (the sequential f32 sum), not a claim about the device |

The table is generated by `tools/fttn/profile_table.py` from `vgpu info --json`; the rules and every card run behind them are in
[../matrix-numerics.md](../matrix-numerics.md), the recorded FP8 vectors in [fp8-vectors.md](fp8-vectors.md), FTTN's rows in
[fttn-simulators.md](fttn-simulators.md).

Notes:

- t4's tf32 chunk is `inferred` (a Turing tensor core has no tf32 form); its f16 numbers are measured.
- "Sequential f32 sum" is `acc += a * b` in f32, k in order, round to nearest even: no card is known to do it. It is what a form gets that
  no measurement, paper or recorded vector covers. `VGPU_TC_EXACT=0` gives it for every form of every device, for the way back to the
  results before 2026-11-15.
- The integer forms (IMMA and the int8 MFMA forms) are exact wrapping sums everywhere, and the f64 form (DMMA) is a chain of IEEE double
  fused multiply-adds (what FTTN's fp64 program prints on every card we ran); neither is in the table.
- sm_120's narrow-float sum (e4m3, e5m2, e3m2, e2m3, e2m1, block-scaled) is the exact sum cut toward zero: 18 of the 27 dense forms on the
  RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition match it bit for bit; nine forms (an e4m3 or e5m2 operand against one spanning many binades, with NaNs or
  infinities among the operands) do not, and no model tried explains them (`nvidia/docs/lowprec.md`).
  The RTX PRO's FP8 differs from the B200's (the B200's vectors give 164 to 183 mismatches of 500 on it): it is not the `via_f16` rule.

## AMD

No AMD matrix-core hardware was available to us, and no AMD profile has a `numerics:` section: nothing on the AMD side is `measured`, and
none of it carries a class in the profile. What the simulator does, and what a paper says of the card:

| Profile | gfx | f16, bf16 MFMA / WMMA | f32 and f64 MFMA | Class | What a paper says |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| mi250x | gfx90a | sum in double, in order, rounded once (default); opt-in `VGPU_MFMA_CDNA2=fttn` is FTTN's MI250X row; opt-in `VGPU_MFMA_MODEL=khattak` is Khattak et al.'s CDNA2 model | double sum / FMA chain | **unknown** (the opt-in rules: published, and they disagree) | FTTN: no subnormals, 3 extra bits, RTN-TE, width 1; MMA-Sim (arXiv 2511.10909): pairs, then fours, each rounded and flushed |
| mi300x, mi325x | gfx942 | sum in double, in order, rounded once; opt-in `VGPU_MFMA_MODEL=khattak` is Khattak et al.'s CDNA3 model | same | **unknown** (the opt-in model: published) | MMA-Sim has a CDNA3 model (a third sum); no FTTN row |
| mi350x, mi455x | gfx950, gfx1250 | sum in double, in order, rounded once | same | **unknown** | no published model |
| rx7900xtx, rx9070xt | gfx1100, gfx1201 | WMMA: sum in double, in order, rounded once | none (RDNA has no f32 or f64 matrix instruction) | **unknown** | none |
| rx6800, rx6700xt, rx6900xt | gfx1030, gfx1031 | none (no matrix instruction) | none | not applicable | |

MI100 (gfx908, CDNA1) has no profile. MMA-Sim's CDNA1, CDNA2 and CDNA3 models are three different algorithms, none the simulator's default, and no
AMD vectors we could check were published, so they stay opt-in or absent until a card or recorded data decides.

Not matrix arithmetic, but also per profile and also mostly unmeasured: the NaN a float instruction returns (payload and sign; measured on gfx1030 only, so
`unknown` on the other six AMD targets, issue #727), the low bits of `v_rcp`, `v_rsq`, `v_sqrt`, `v_exp`, `v_log`, `v_sin`, `v_cos` (the host's on all
targets; measured on gfx1030 and gfx1031, issue #729), and the f64 forms of the first three (exact in the simulator, 24 bits on gfx1030 and gfx1031, issue #728).
[../amd-cross-arch.md](../amd-cross-arch.md) lists what the targets do differently from one another and which side was right.
